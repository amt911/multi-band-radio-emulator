## Design Context

> Starter design brief for the impeccable skills — refine as the identity firms up. Android app that emulates radio time-signal protocols (DCF77, etc.) through the phone's audio.

### Users
Hobbyists and tinkerers who want to **set radio-controlled clocks/watches without the real transmitter**, by playing an emulated time-signal through audio. They pick a protocol, start playback, and expect the target device to lock. Technically literate; they value precision and clear feedback.

### Brand Personality
**Precise · Instrument-like · Honest.** This is a tool/instrument, not a toy. It should feel accurate and dependable — closer to test equipment than to a flashy consumer app.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: Functional, console/instrument feel. Dark-friendly, calm surfaces; a single accent for the active/transmitting state. Material 3.
- **State is the hero**: which protocol is selected, playing vs. stopped, and the encoded time must be unmistakable at a glance.
- **Typography**: a clean sans plus a mono/tabular treatment for time and signal data.
- **Not**: playful/gamey styling, neon, decorative motion. Restraint reads as accuracy.

### Design Principles
1. **Unambiguous state.** Transmitting vs. idle and the selected protocol are always obvious.
2. **Precision over polish.** The UI signals trustworthiness like an instrument.
3. **One accent = "live".** Reserve the strong color for the active/transmitting state.
4. **Clear protocol selection.** Each `AntennaType` is easy to find, label, and start/stop.
5. **Accessible controls.** Large targets; Compose `semantics`/`contentDescription` on every control.
